\begin{abstract}

Modern quantum foundations increasingly recognize that instantaneous state descriptions are
insufficient to account for contextuality, non-Markovian dynamics, and intervention-dependent
behavior. Related advances extend the descriptive object to non-Markovian and multi-time process
formalisms, yet typically retain the assumption that a suitably defined algebraic object can
remain sufficient for prediction under interventions.

In this work we identify and formalize a structural diagnostic underlying these phenomena, which
we term \emph{the unfaithful cut}. We consider many-to-one operational reductions from a space of
admissible histories to reduced descriptions such as states, channels, or static multi-time
process tensors. We introduce a criterion of \emph{faithfulness with respect to interventions},
requiring that identical reduced descriptions permit identical sets of admissible future
continuations under a specified class of physical interventions.

We show that, under physically standard conditions, reductions that rely on algebraically closed
descriptive objects—whether instantaneous states or static multi-time process tensors
($\Sigma_1$ descriptions)—can fail to be faithful: distinct underlying histories may map to the
same reduced object yet support different counterfactual responses to later interventions. This
failure is representational rather than dynamical and cannot, in general, be repaired by
modifying equations of motion on the reduced space alone.

We then characterize the minimal \emph{representational structure} required to restore
counterfactual semantics, formalized here as a trajectory-level admissibility geometry
($\Sigma_2$). In this framework, reduced descriptions are associated with structured families of
admissible histories rather than single points, and memory effects or arrow-like directedness
arise as geometric consequences of reduction rather than as fundamental dynamical postulates.
Our results apply across state-based, process-theoretic, and causal-model frameworks and clarify
the structural limits of algebraic reductions in open quantum systems and quantum control.

\end{abstract}
